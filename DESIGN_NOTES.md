# Campaign design notes

## Research

Nintendo's Super Mario Bros. Wonder developer interview discusses freedom in
course progression, ways to reduce discouragement, and abilities that change
how players approach a course:
https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2023/October/Ask-the-Developer-Vol-11-Super-Mario-Bros-Wonder-Chapter-3-2461479.html

Its first chapter describes transformations that change the course and play:
https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-chapter-1/

Nintendo's 3D World character guide describes movement differences and Rosalina's
spin attack:
https://supermario3dworld.nintendo.com/play-together/

These are design references, not claims of identical mechanics or production quality.

## Applied decisions

- Give each stop one dominant mechanic, introduced before its hardest section.
- Connect story objectives to actions in the level: refill, release, reverse,
  recover, then stop the engine.
- Keep dialogue brief and let the player advance it.
- Make medals optional so progression does not require repeated backtracking.
- Preserve completed objectives at checkpoints; use immediate retries.
- Provide jump buffering, a short ledge grace period, variable jump height,
  and a rechargeable burst for a second way to handle enemies.
- Use one consistent modeled character and a limited color palette per location.

## Scope

Five short authored stages, a route menu, local stage-unlock saving, optional
medals, and a final boss. The game uses 3D meshes with side-scrolling controls.
The geometry and character animation are deliberately simple; this is a playable
fan-game build, not a recreation of a Nintendo production's art assets.
